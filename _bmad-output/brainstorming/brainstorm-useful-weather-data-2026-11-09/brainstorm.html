Know your materials.

Evidence before certainty

Raw fields exist for temperature, humidity, pressure, wind, UV, solar radiation, illuminance, rain, precipitation type, lightning reports and battery. Their presence does not prove completeness, accuracy or query availability.

Observation-led

What was measured?

Fresh conditions, gust episodes and sensor reports. Keep timestamps and source context visible; historical observations describe what happened.

Derived / comparative

What can it explain?

Local-day totals, true extrema, event boundaries, comparable periods and formula-based measures need explicit methods and usable coverage.

Additional input / source

What else is needed?

Personal thresholds, diaries and photos need user input. Forecasts, a second station, indoor sensors or electricity-generation records add dependencies.

Idea tags below are indicative coach screening, not a feasibility audit. Categories overlap: an observation-led card can still need aggregation, and a derived feature can need an extra source.

The emerging shape · coach synthesis

An answer at the surface.
A casebook underneath.

The strongest connection is a product with three linked layers: a useful briefing, historical context and an inspectable trail of evidence.

01What happened?

A short observed-weather briefing. Useful before choosing a metric or date.

02What stands out?

Explain unusual conditions against comparable, complete station periods.

03How do we know?

Open the source period, calculation and coverage; use a chart when it helps investigate.

A weather detective experience could make the station’s history distinctive even when another app already supplies daily conditions. Personal memory, event replay and comparisons are promising hypotheses.

The notebook’s guardrails.

These checks decide what can honestly be claimed, before records, comparisons or alerts are promised.

Periods, rainfall and true extremes

Current daily refinement targets UTC days; local summaries need Europe/London boundaries. Verify units, rainfall interval semantics and possible duplication. High and low values in an averaged series are not true temperature extremes. Derive time above thresholds and measured solar energy using the correct intervals. Weight daily averages appropriately when coverage differs.

Coverage, history and station records

Verify history length, usable periods, ingestion cadence, sensor gaps and access to raw fields. Missing data can break or qualify a spell. Short histories support station-history comparisons, not climatological normals or climate-change claims. A station record is not a regional or all-time record.

Freshness, forecasts and practical interpretation

Scheduled ingestion plus Athena has not been established as timely enough for alerts. Future activity windows need an explicit forecast source. Observations support recent conditions and historical tendencies. Comfort and drying proxies need explained assumptions and validation. Outdoor readings do not establish soil moisture or indoor conditions; no health or safety reliance is proposed.
